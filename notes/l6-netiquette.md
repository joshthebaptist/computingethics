## Slide 4

- What Is the Internet?
- A worldwide “network of networks” in which users at one computer can, with permission, get information from any other computer.
- Who created it?
- Conceived by the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) of the US government. Its first network, ARPANET, went live in 1969.
- Original aim
- To let researchers using a computer at one university “talk to” research computers at other universities.

## Slide 5

- From ARPANET to the AI Era
- 1969
- ARPANET
- First messages sent between US universities
- 1983
- TCP/IP
- Common protocol: the modern Internet is born
- 1991
- World Wide Web
- Tim Berners-Lee (CERN) makes the Web public
- 1993
- Web browser
- Mosaic makes the Web easy and visual
- 1995
- Commercial Internet
- Business and advertising open up
- 2007+
- Smartphones & social
- Internet in every pocket
- 2022+
- Generative AI
- Chatbots and AI content go mainstream
- Early Internet: technical and hard to use, limited to military, corporate and academic institutions, and advertising was not allowed. The Web and browsers made it easy — and used by everyone.

## Slide 10

- What Is Netiquette?
- net + etiquette
- Etiquette for the Internet: widely accepted guidelines for appropriate online behaviour
- Applies to e-mail, forums, chat, file sharing and the Web — and today to messaging apps, social media and video calls
- Norms are rarely universal: they depend on each social group and culture, both real and virtual
- Where it came from
- Early networks let researchers exchange data and documents
- Users soon used them for quick, informal social chat
- Rules grew up on bulletin boards and Usenet newsgroups
- Written down in Virginia Shea’s book Netiquette (1994) and the IETF’s RFC 1855, Netiquette Guidelines (1995)

## Slide 12

- Netiquette: Browsing the Internet
- Browse wisely
- Use reliable search engines and check sources before trusting them
- Organise bookmarks and reading lists
- Avoid subversive or pornographic sites — they harm individual, organisational and national reputation
- Filter content yourself: the Internet is borderless, so users are trusted to self-censor
- Browse responsibly
- Don’t spread viruses — scan downloads
- Respect copyright and IP; credit the owner
- Avoid negative or toxic groups and forums
- Handle sensitive issues with care — in Malaysia, the “3R”: race, religion and royalty

## Slide 13

- Netiquette: File Transfer and Sharing
- Read the terms
- Check the provider’s rules before uploading or downloading, and follow service and e-transaction agreements
- Share links carefully
- Set cloud-sharing permissions (view only, named people) and remove access when no longer needed
- Be considerate
- Transfer very large files outside busy times, and make sure there is enough storage
- Respect copyright
- Don’t share illegal, pirated or controversial material
- Share only the truth
- Don’t spread false statements or accusations; make sure what you share is accurate
- Follow security policy
- Don’t steal data; follow your organisation’s security rules

## Slide 15

- Bad E-mail Examples: What Went Wrong?
- BOTH ARE BAD
- To: Group@massmails.com
- Subject: Attn Marc
- THIS IS FOR MARC ONLY AND IF YOU ARE NOT HIM DELETE. MARC THE BALLGAME THE OTHER DAY WAS JUST SWEET!!!! — TIM
- To: Members@thelist.com
- Subject: Officers Meeting (officers only)
- We will have a meeting this Thursday at McDonald’s to go over this month’s community service projects. — Bob (President)
- Personal message sent to a whole group
- Fills others’ inboxes; may be read by rival teams
- ALL CAPS and too many “!!!!” read as shouting
- “Officers only” sent to all members
- Non-officers may skim it and turn up
- Use a separate list or address officers directly

## Slide 16

- Good E-mail Examples: What Went Right?
- BOTH ARE GOOD
- To: Group@massmail.com
- Subject: Problems at the club office
- Construction has cut the power at the office. If you planned to work on projects there, please try next week. — Jan
- To: Tech@ga4h.org
- Subject: The web page assignments
- The deadline for these assignments is *June 24th*. Please have them completed and uploaded by then. — Robbie
- Useful to every member
- Meant for everyone on the list
- No personal remarks; group jokes are fine
- A clear deadline e-mail
- Relevant to the whole club
- Key points highlighted

## Slide 17

- Putting It All Together: Who Gets the Job?
- Same content — different impression
- i’ve attached my resume i would be grateful if you could read it and get back to me at your earliest convenience. i have all the experience you are looking for – i’ve worked in a customer-facing environment for three years, i am competent with ms office and i enjoy working as part of a team. thanks for your time
- Dear Sir/Madam,
- I have attached my resume. I would be grateful if you could read it and reply at your earliest convenience. I have the experience you are looking for:
- a. Three years in a customer-facing role
- b. Competent with Microsoft Office
- c. I enjoy working in a team
- Yours faithfully,
- Joe Bloggs
- No greeting, no capitals, one long sentence
- Greeting, structure, list, formal sign-off

## Slide 18

- E-mail Tips (1)
- E-MAIL
- Use BCC for bulk e-mail
- Put recipients in BCC to protect their privacy and stop spammers harvesting addresses
- Don’t shout
- No ALL CAPS, oversized fonts or threats
- Avoid “Reply All”
- Only include people who really need to see your reply
- Remember attachments
- Tip: attach the file before you write the message
- Use priority correctly
- Don’t mark normal e-mails as “high priority”

## Slide 19

- E-mail Tips (2)
- E-MAIL
- Meaningful subject line
- Summarise the message; update the subject when replying if the topic changes
- Check before sending
- Correct punctuation, grammar and spelling — and the recipient’s name
- Formal sign-off
- “Yours sincerely” if you know their name; “Yours faithfully” after “Dear Sir/Madam”
- Everyday sign-off
- “Best regards” or “Kind regards”; with friends, “Take care” or “Have a nice day”
- Use a professional address
- For study and work, use your university or work e-mail, not a nickname account

## Slide 20

- E-mail Tips (3)
- E-MAIL
- Reply within 24 hours
- Prompt replies are polite and look efficient; even a short “received, will reply by Friday” helps
- Allow time for a reply
- Wait a few days before sending a polite reminder — not everyone is online all day
- Long content goes in a file
- Send long documents as an attachment or shared link, not in the e-mail body
- Use emoji and acronyms wisely
- Fine for informal, personal messages — avoid them in formal e-mails
- Check AI-drafted e-mails
- If AI helps you write, check facts, names and tone before you press send

## Slide 21

- Auto-Replies and Signatures
- E-MAIL
- Out-of-office replies
- Use an automatic reply when on leave or away for a long time
- Say when you will return and who to contact meanwhile
- Don’t announce holiday details publicly — it can be a security risk
- A sensible signature
- Name, position, organisation and contact number
- Keep it short — long quotes and images clutter replies
- Avoid sharing your home address

## Slide 22

- Managing Your Mailbox
- Check your inbox often
- for urgent messages
- Scan attachments
- never forward a virus
- Use your address book
- avoid mistyped recipients
- Protect private data
- don’t send or keep sensitive details in e-mail
- Keep it tidy
- archive or delete old mail; share large files by link
- Spot phishing
- check the sender, links and urgency before clicking

## Slide 23

- Mailing Lists and Discussion Groups
- The same rules apply to forums, Telegram channels and Discord servers
- Before and while posting
- Join groups that are relevant and useful
- Observe (“lurk”) before you take part
- Trim old replies from long threads
- Proofread for clarity, spelling and grammar
- THINK before posting
- Being a good member
- Don’t join too many groups
- Stay on topic and share experiences
- Explain context for readers from different backgrounds
- Be polite
- Don’t be a troll or “keyboard warrior”

## Slide 25

- Group Chat Etiquette (WhatsApp, Telegram)
- NEW
- Do
- Keep messages on topic for the group
- Message people privately for personal matters
- Respect time: avoid late-night messages to class or work groups
- Ask before adding someone to a group
- Don’t
- Forward chain messages or unverified “viral” news
- Spam stickers, voice notes or memes in formal groups
- Share screenshots of private chats without consent
- Post others’ phone numbers or photos

## Slide 26

- Video Meeting Etiquette (Zoom, Teams, Meet)
- NEW
- Join on time
- Test your audio and camera first
- Mute when not speaking
- Avoid background noise
- Camera on when expected
- Use a tidy or blurred background
- Use the chat and “raise hand”
- Don’t talk over others
- Ask before recording
- Recording without consent breaks trust — and may break the law
- Protect meeting links
- Don’t post links publicly (“Zoombombing”)

## Slide 27

- Social Media and Your Digital Footprint
- NEW
- Everything you post can be screenshotted, shared and found years later
- Employers and universities often check applicants’ social media
- Think about who can see a post — and who they might share it with
- Don’t post photos of others, especially children, without permission
- Disagree respectfully; don’t pile on in online “cancel” campaigns
- Ask yourself
- “Would I be happy for my family, lecturer or future boss to see this?”

## Slide 28

- Fake News, Deepfakes and AI
- NEW
- Check before you share
- Verify with trusted news sources or MCMC’s fact-check portal, sebenarnya.my
- Look for the original source and date
- Be wary of emotional, urgent “share now!” posts
- Deepfake videos and cloned voices are now common in scams
- Using AI politely and honestly
- Say when content is AI-generated — especially in assignments
- Don’t paste others’ private information into AI tools
- Check AI output for errors before sharing
- Never create fake images of real people

## Slide 29

- When Bad Netiquette Becomes a Crime (Malaysia)
- NEW
- Penal Code (Amendment) Act 2025
- In force 11 July 2025. Sections 507B–507G make bullying, including cyberbullying, harassment, threats and doxing (sharing someone’s personal information), criminal offences — up to 10 years’ jail in the most serious cases.
- Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, s. 233
- Offence to make or share offensive, false or menacing content online.
- Online Safety Act 2025
- In force 1 January 2026. Large platforms must reduce harmful content, especially for children. The government has also announced a minimum age of 16 for social media accounts, with ID-based age checks.
