## Slide 4

- The Evolution of Ethics Theory
- Socrates
- c. 470–399 BC
- Moral values and virtue; “know thyself”
- Plato
- c. 428–348 BC
- Moral values + learning: knowledge leads to virtue
- Aristotle
- 384–322 BC
- Virtue ethics: good character built by habit; aims at a good end (telos)
- Immanuel Kant
- 1785
- Deontology: act from duty, whatever the outcome
- Bentham & Mill
- 1789 / 1861
- Utilitarianism: the greatest happiness for the greatest number
- ANCIENT GREEK
- MODERN

## Slide 5

- Recap: Two Main Ethics Theories
- Teleology
- Focuses on consequences
- Main approach: utilitarianism
- An action is ethical if it produces the most happiness and least harm for the most people
- Deontology
- Focuses on duty and responsibility
- Main approach: Kantian ethics
- An action is ethical if it follows moral duty, even when it does not produce the most happiness
- Codes of ethics in ICT: last week — codes for professionals; this week — ethical issues for everyday system users and organisations.

## Slide 6

- Why Do We Need to Learn Computer Ethics?
- As guidance
- To build discipline
- To use ICT to its full advantage
- Good values and positive attitudes
- To improve moral quality
- An informed society with positive values
- A productive work environment
- To avoid social problems linked to ICT

## Slide 7

- Why Is Computer Ethics Hard for the Public?
- People cannot see the social impact and problems of ICT
- Many have little knowledge of how technology works
- Few know the cyberlaws that apply to them
- Harm is often indirect — people are not personally affected when issues arise
- Little exposure to the influence, power and use of computers
- New today: AI systems work as “black boxes”, and data collection happens invisibly in the background
- Think about it
- How many of the apps on your phone can see your location right now — and did you choose that?

## Slide 10

- 1. Economy and Social Issues
- DILEMMA 1 OF 10
- Jobs created and lost
- Technology creates new jobs but displaces others. The WEF projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030 — a net gain of 78 million.
- Easier work, more pressure
- Tasks become easier, but “always-on” e-mail and messaging blur work and home life, straining families.
- The digital divide
- ICT brings opportunity, but not for all: 2.2 billion people were still offline in 2025, mostly in low- and middle-income countries.
- Source: World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025; ITU, Facts and Figures 2025

## Slide 11

- 2. Personal Routines · 3. Software Development
- DILEMMAS 2–3 OF 10
- 2. Personal routines
- Protect accounts: strong passwords, multi-factor authentication or passkeys
- Back up data (e.g. the 3-2-1 rule)
- Be aware of privacy settings and what you share
- Handle printed copies of sensitive data carefully
- 3. Software development
- Quality depends on the developer’s expertise, trustworthiness and moral values
- Ethics should guide good conduct, e.g. testing and honest reporting
- Some users cannot afford licences and use pirated software — free and open-source alternatives are a legal option

## Slide 12

- 4. Management and Staff
- DILEMMA 4 OF 10
- Dilemmas arise from differences in power and authority.
- Disgruntled staff
- An unhappy employee destroys programs or data, or plants a “logic bomb” before leaving. Insider threats remain a major cause of data breaches.
- Pressure from above
- A manager pushes staff to break rules when writing programs. Example: Volkswagen engineers wrote “defeat device” software to cheat emissions tests (exposed 2015).

## Slide 13

- 5. Computer Processing Issues
- DILEMMA 5 OF 10
- Untrustworthy systems
- Operations rely on untrusted software and hardware
- Services cannot be delivered when they fail
- Delays in delivering output
- Late results put pressure on clients
- Example: a faulty CrowdStrike update (July 2024) crashed about 8.5 million Windows devices, grounding flights and delaying hospital services

## Slide 14

- 6. Work Environment · 7. Computer Crime
- DILEMMAS 6–7 OF 10
- 6. Employee monitoring
- Is it ethical for an employer to monitor staff phone calls, location, keystrokes or screens?
- Remote work since COVID-19 has made “bossware” more common
- Balance: security and productivity vs privacy and trust; be transparent and proportionate
- 7. Computer crime
- Fraud, scams, hoaxes, ransomware and identity theft
- Many crimes go unreported, and they are increasing and becoming more complex — now helped by AI deepfakes
- Malaysia: Computer Crimes Act 1997; Online Safety Act 2025

## Slide 15

- 8. Data Collection, Storage and Access
- DILEMMA 8 OF 10
- Concerns privacy, confidentiality and accuracy
- Should we collect all the information we can, or only what is necessary? (data minimisation)
- Get permission (consent) from the data owner
- How do we make sure data is accurate, precise and true?
- Malaysia PDPA 2010: seven principles
- General
- Notice & choice
- Disclosure
- Security
- Retention
- Data integrity
- Access

## Slide 16

- 9. Resource Exploitation · 10. Client and Vendor
- DILEMMAS 9–10 OF 10
- 9. Resource exploitation
- Disrupting services (e.g. denial-of-service attacks)
- Exploiting system vulnerabilities
- Using others’ computing power without permission, e.g. cryptojacking
- 10. Client and vendor
- Vendor: provides services and receives payment
- Client: pays to receive services
- Issue: the vendor fails to deliver quality output or gives poor service — or the client withholds payment

## Slide 18

- Four Ethical Issues of the Information Age
- Richard O. Mason, MIS Quarterly (1986) — known as PAPA
- Privacy
- What information about ourselves must we reveal, and under what safeguards?
- Accuracy
- Who is responsible for the accuracy of information, and for errors?
- Property
- Who owns information and intellectual property, and what is a fair price?
- Accessibility
- Who has the right to access information, and on what terms?
- Aspects that often create problems for computing professionals
- Misconduct
- Privacy
- Accountability
- Licensing
- Ownership

## Slide 19

- The Privacy Aspect
- When does conflict arise?
- When databases store private information, e.g. for marketing and targeted advertising
- What form does it take?
- Obeying company policy vs meeting the client’s or data subject’s expectations
- What should we do?
- Weigh the benefits and consequences for the data owner if their information is kept or shared — and follow consent and PDPA rules

## Slide 20

- The Accountability Aspect
- Professionals are responsible for security and correct operation at every stage of an application’s life cycle.
- 1
- Planning
- 2
- Design
- 3
- Implementation
- 4
- Testing
- 5
- Documentation
- Management must also plan testing and release: service and maintenance releases, beta versions and patches — and be honest about known bugs.

## Slide 21

- Software Fails — Who Is Responsible?
- Programmer
- Manager
- Dealer / vendor
- User
- Users may not fully understand what the software can and cannot do.
- The problem of “many hands”
- When many people contribute, it is easy for everyone to avoid blame (Nissenbaum, 1996).
- The law is catching up
- The EU’s new Product Liability Directive (2024) treats software, including AI, as a product, so makers can be liable for defects.

## Slide 22

- The Licensing Aspect
- Society needs assurance that professionals practise ethically
- Why licensing?
- Builds confidence, trust and efficiency in professional work and its output
- Requires professionals to follow quality guidelines
- Discourages misconduct — candidates must pass exams and meet standards, and a licence can be revoked
- Examples: medical licences, professional certificates, product certification
- Licensing in Malaysian ICT today
- MBOT registers Professional Technologists (Ts.) under Act 768 (2015)
- Cyber Security Act 2024: providers of penetration testing and managed SOC monitoring must hold a NACSA licence (from 26 August 2024)
- Industry certificates such as CISSP or cloud certifications show competence

## Slide 24

- Making an Ethical Decision
- Analyse the scenario from three perspectives
- Ethics and law
- Is it legal? Is it ethical? The two do not always match.
- Formal and informal reasons
- Is the reason based on policy and law, or on personal judgement?
- Principles and theories
- What do ethical principles and theories say?

## Slide 25

- Formal and Informal Reasons
- Formal reasons
- Linked to policy and law
- e.g. “The PDPA forbids sharing this data without consent”
- e.g. “Company policy bans personal use of admin accounts”
- Informal reasons
- Individual and isolated, not linked to policy or law
- e.g. “It just feels wrong to read her messages”
- e.g. “My friend would be upset”

## Slide 26

- Ethical Principles as Guidance
- Universality
- An action is ethical if it would still be acceptable when every member of society did the same, for the same shared interest (Kant).
- Justice
- An action is ethical if it brings justice and fairness to the public.

## Slide 27

- Rights and Responsibilities
- A = software developer · B = client
- A’s responsibility
- Develop quality software
- A’s right
- Receive payment
- B’s right
- Receive high-quality software
- B’s responsibility
- Pay, and do not use pirated copies
- Every right creates a matching duty for someone else.
