## Slide 4

- Recap: What Is Ethics?
- A branch of philosophy that addresses questions of morality — good and bad, noble and ignoble, right and wrong, justice and virtue.
- A discipline
- It investigates principles, methods and human behaviour.
- Shaped by context
- How ethics is applied depends on external factors such as culture, profession and technology.

## Slide 5

- How ICT Has Changed Ethics
- ICT has changed both the criteria and the nature of ethics
- 01
- Before ICT
- Debated mainly from theoretical and philosophical perspectives: life, the Creator and nature.
- 02
- ICT era
- Exercised through action: everyday choices online, in software and in data handling.
- 03
- AI era (today)
- Decisions are delegated to algorithms and AI, raising new questions of bias, accountability and transparency.

## Slide 6

- Why Ethics from Other Fields Is Not Enough for ICT
- Speed
- Fast computer communication leads people to act before thinking about the consequences
- Easy copying
- Digital content is easily modified, accessed and copied: hacking, plagiarism, privacy breaches, piracy
- Built-in conflict
- ICT aims to give access to information, yet that same access exposes information to threats
- Weak verification
- Limited access control and verification make unethical use easier
- Today: AI deepfakes, ransomware and large-scale data breaches make these problems far bigger.
- Source: Parker, Swope & Baker (1990)

## Slide 8

- Why ICT Needs a Code of Ethics
- A code of ethics guides responsibility and good values for everyone who builds or uses ICT.
- It should cover…
- How users are expected to act
- How the organisation anticipates users’ behaviour and use of computers
- How professionals work
- Standards for the people who design, build and run systems
- Acknowledgement
- All rules and conduct must be made known and accepted by everyone

## Slide 9

- How Are the Rules Shared?
- The approach varies between organisations
- The objective is always to prevent misconduct
- Rules are usually signed or accepted when joining, and refreshed through regular training
- Policies & codes of practice
- e.g. Acceptable Use Policy, social media policy, AI use policy
- Standards
- e.g. ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management)

## Slide 10

- Principles of Computer Ethics
- Balance
- The benefits of a system should outweigh its risks
- Informed consent
- People affected by ICT should know about it, be ready for it and agree to it
- Justice
- Share benefits and burdens fairly; those who gain the advantages take responsibility
- Minimise risk
- Avoid any unnecessary or irrelevant risk

## Slide 11

- Two Types of Codes of Ethics in ICT
- For professionals
- Guide the people who design, build, manage and secure systems
- ACM Code of Ethics (2018)
- Software Engineering Code (1999)
- MBOT Code of Ethics (Malaysia)
- For system users
- Guide everyone who uses an organisation’s systems
- Acceptable Use Policies
- University ICT rules
- Platform community guidelines

## Slide 12

- The Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics
- Computer Ethics Institute (Barquin, 1992)
- 1
- Do not use a computer to harm other people.
- 2
- Do not interfere with other people’s computer work.
- 3
- Do not snoop around in other people’s computer files.
- 4
- Do not use a computer to steal.
- 5
- Do not use a computer to bear false witness.
- 6
- Do not copy or use proprietary software you have not paid for.
- 7
- Do not use others’ computer resources without authorisation or proper compensation.
- 8
- Do not appropriate other people’s intellectual output.
- 9
- Think about the social consequences of the program you write or the system you design.
- 10
- Always use a computer in ways that show consideration and respect for others.

## Slide 14

- Why Do Professionals Need Ethics?
- Shared values
- Values and good behaviour that each member of a profession must embrace
- Clear direction
- Sets out the right actions to follow and the wrong actions to avoid
- Group standards
- Evaluates moral principles for a group, to guide how members perform their work
- Integrity
- Protects each professional’s moral integrity

## Slide 15

- A social-control tool that helps members discipline themselves to be more ethical
- Inspiration & guidance
- Prevention & discipline of misconduct
- Preserving professional integrity
- Preserving harmony
- Giving support to members
- Building a positive public image

## Slide 17

- Association for Computing Machinery (ACM)
- Founded 1947; the world’s largest computing society
- Its Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (latest version 2018) guides members on development and use of systems
- Each principle comes with guidelines explaining how to apply it
- Violations can lead to penalties, including loss of membership
- Other professional bodies

- BCS
- BCS, The Chartered Institute for IT (UK)
- CIPS
- Canadian Information Processing Society
- DPMA
- Data Processing Management Association (renamed AITP in 1996)
- MBOT

## Slide 18

- The ACM Code: Purpose (Preamble)
- Who it is for
- All computing professionals — practitioners, instructors, students, influencers — and anyone who uses computing in an impactful way
- What it does
- Inspires and guides ethical conduct, and serves as a basis for remedies when violations occur
- How it is written
- Principles stated as responsibilities, each supported by guidelines that explain how to apply them
- Core idea
- The public good is always the primary consideration
- Source: ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018), acm.org/code-of-ethics

## Slide 19

- ACM Section 1: General Ethical Principles
- The foundation for the rest of the Code
- 1.1
- Contribute to society and to human well-being, acknowledging that all people are stakeholders in computing
- 1.2
- Avoid harm
- 1.3
- Be honest and trustworthy
- 1.4
- Be fair and take action not to discriminate
- 1.5
- Respect the work required to produce new ideas, inventions, creative works and computing artifacts
- 1.6
- Respect privacy
- 1.7
- Honor confidentiality
- Source: ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018)

## Slide 20

- ACM Section 2: Professional Responsibilities
- 2.1
- Strive for high quality in both the processes and products of professional work
- 2.2
- Maintain high standards of professional competence, conduct and ethical practice
- 2.3
- Know and respect existing rules pertaining to professional work
- 2.4
- Accept and provide appropriate professional review
- 2.5
- Give thorough evaluations of computer systems and their impacts, including possible risks
- 2.6
- Perform work only in areas of competence
- 2.7
- Foster public awareness and understanding of computing and its consequences
- 2.8
- Access computing resources only when authorised or when compelled by the public good
- 2.9
- Design and implement systems that are robustly and usably secure
- Source: ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018)

## Slide 21

- ACM Section 3: Professional Leadership Principles
- For those who lead, at work or as volunteers
- 3.1
- Ensure the public good is the central concern during all professional computing work
- 3.2
- Articulate, encourage and evaluate the social responsibilities of members
- 3.3
- Manage personnel and resources to enhance the quality of working life
- 3.4
- Articulate, apply and support policies and processes that reflect the Code
- 3.5
- Create opportunities for members to grow as professionals
- 3.6
- Use care when modifying or retiring systems
- 3.7
- Recognize and take special care of systems that become part of society’s infrastructure
- Source: ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018)

## Slide 22

- ACM Section 4: Compliance with the Code
- 4.1
- Uphold, promote and respect the principles of the Code
- 4.2
- Treat violations of the Code as inconsistent with ACM membership
- How to use the Code
- It is not an algorithm for solving ethical problems — it is a basis for ethical decision-making
- Several principles may apply to one issue, with different weight
- The public good is the paramount consideration
- Open, transparent discussion makes the profession accountable to all stakeholders

## Slide 23

- The Software Engineering Code of Ethics
- Joint ACM / IEEE Computer Society code, version 5.2, adopted 1999
- Developed by a joint ACM and IEEE Computer Society task force
- Contains 8 principles on the behaviour and decisions of software engineers
- Applies to practitioners, educators, managers, supervisors, policy makers, trainees and students
- Why it matters
- Software engineers can do good or cause harm, and can enable or influence others to do the same.

## Slide 24

- The Software Engineering Code: Preamble
- Who are software engineers?
- Anyone who contributes, directly or by teaching, to the analysis, specification, design, development, certification, maintenance and testing of software systems
- Their commitment
- To make software engineering a beneficial and respected profession
- Their duty
- To the health, safety and welfare of the public, by following the eight principles
- Source: ACM/IEEE-CS Software Engineering Code of Ethics and Professional Practice (1999)

## Slide 25

- The Eight Principles
- 1
- Public
- Act consistently with the public interest
- 2
- Client & Employer
- Act in their best interests, consistent with the public interest
- 3
- Product
- Meet the highest professional standards possible
- 4
- Judgment
- Keep integrity and independence in professional judgment
- 5
- Management
- Managers promote ethical software management
- 6
- Profession
- Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession
- 7
- Colleagues
- Be fair to and supportive of colleagues
- 8
- Self
- Pursue lifelong learning and promote ethical practice
- Source: ACM/IEEE-CS Software Engineering Code (1999), ethics.acm.org

## Slide 27

- Professional Ethics in Malaysia
- Malaysia Board of Technologists (MBOT)
- Set up under the Technologists and Technicians Act 2015 (Act 768), in force 1 August 2015
- Registers Professional Technologists (Ts.) and Certified Technicians
- Has a Code of Ethics; the Board can suspend or cancel registration for misconduct
- Key laws ICT professionals must know
- Personal Data Protection Act 2010
- Amended 2024: breach notification and data protection officers
- Cyber Security Act 2024
- Duties for national critical information infrastructure
- Online Safety Act 2025
- In force 1 Jan 2026: platform safety duties
- Computer Crimes Act 1997
- Unauthorised access and modification

## Slide 28

- When Professional Ethics Fails: Real Cases
- UK Post Office Horizon
- 1999–2015
- Faulty accounting software led to 900+ sub-postmasters being prosecuted; many convictions were quashed by law in 2024.
- Principles: ACM 1.2, 1.3, 2.5
- Boeing 737 MAX
- 2018–2019
- Flight-control software (MCAS) contributed to two crashes that killed 346 people.
- Principles: SE Code 1 & 3; ACM 2.5
- CrowdStrike outage
- July 2024
- A faulty security update crashed about 8.5 million Windows devices, disrupting airlines, banks and hospitals.
- Principles: ACM 2.1, 3.7
